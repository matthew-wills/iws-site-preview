# Summary

On 18/06/2026, Saltbush Air SB512, a twin-turboprop registered VH-SBQ, began its take-off from runway 09 at Tarlton Springs Aerodrome. The crew saw two kangaroos on the runway and rejected the take-off. The aircraft struck both animals and stopped 62 m into the runway end safety area. None of the 47 people on board was seriously injured, although one cabin crew member reported bruising. The aircraft was substantially damaged and both kangaroos were killed. The aerodrome was closed for 4 hours 20 minutes.

The investigation is examining how the animals reached the runway, runway inspection after last light, the approval of the evening service, and the rejected take-off and stop. Two further questions remain open. The first is whether the crew were fatigued. The second is whether four edge lights, measured above nominal intensity, affected when the animals were seen.

In the interim, the operator suspended the evening service on 19/06/2026 and has listed Tarlton Springs for runway inspection after last light. The aerodrome operator, for its part, is replacing the 140 m section of its northern boundary fence.

# What we know

On 18/06/2026 Saltbush Air SB512, a 50-seat twin-turboprop registered VH-SBQ, was to depart Tarlton Springs Aerodrome for Port Carrick on the last scheduled sector of the day. On board were 44 passengers, 2 flight crew and 1 cabin crew. It was the fifth sector of the flight crew's duty. The evening rotation had been added to that duty the same morning, after a member of the rostered crew reported unfit. The aerodrome's only runway inspection that day was made at 21:35 UTC on 17/06/2026, before the first arrival, and it recorded the movement area as clear.

The doors closed at 07:16 UTC, on schedule. Tarlton Springs has no control tower. The crew therefore made their own broadcasts on the common traffic advisory frequency (CTAF), and they switched on the pilot-activated runway lighting at 07:21:50. The aircraft lined up on runway 09 in darkness, with the AWIS wind at 090 at 6 kt. Neither the crew nor the aerodrome raised a runway inspection. The first officer was flying. The take-off roll began at 07:30:02 (17:30 local), and the captain called V1 at 112 kt at 07:30:29.

At 07:30:31 the first officer called two kangaroos moving onto the runway from the northern side, past the 1,100 m point. Their range at first sight is estimated at around 200 m. One second later the captain took control and rejected the take-off at 118 kt, six knots above V1. Flight data places the aircraft 1,152 m along the runway at that moment, with 695 m of sealed surface remaining. About one second after that, the right main landing gear and the No. 2 propeller struck both animals. Maximum braking was established at 07:30:34. Reverse was then selected on No. 1 only, because No. 2 reverse did not engage.

The aircraft left the end of runway 09 at approximately 21 kt at 07:30:51. It stopped at 07:30:57, 62 m into the 90 m runway end safety area and 28 m short of its end, upright and on the extended centreline. There was no fire. The captain shut down both engines and did not command an evacuation. At the captain's request on the CTAF, the aerodrome reporting officer activated the aerodrome emergency plan, sent a vehicle and closed the aerodrome. Passengers left by the forward airstair. The occurrence notification puts this at 4 minutes 8 seconds after the aircraft stopped, and the CVR records the airstair operating at 07:34:40.

Three No. 2 propeller blades were damaged beyond limits, and that engine needs a shock-load inspection. The right main gear doors were distorted. Both right main tyres deflated through their fuse plugs, and the brakes absorbed 84% of their certified maximum energy. The lower right fuselage was punctured. Nobody was seriously injured. One cabin crew member reported bruising during the disembarkation. Both kangaroos were killed, and the aerodrome was closed for 4 hours 20 minutes.

# What we are looking at

1. How the animals reached the runway. The investigation is examining the northern perimeter fence and the aerodrome's use of its wildlife sighting log. Works register item WR-221 recorded the fence as failed in 04/2023, and the repair was deferred at three budget rounds. An animal pad through the fence at the 1,050 m mark (Figure 1) lies 138 m before the first carcass, on the same side of the runway.

2. Wildlife requirements for aerodromes of this size. Tarlton Springs falls below both thresholds in AS-139 10.4.2. It was therefore not required to hold a documented wildlife hazard management plan, and it held none. The investigation is examining what that threshold means given a logged history of kangaroos on the movement area.

3. Runway inspection after last light. Operations Manual Part A 8.14 requires an inspection only at aerodromes listed in Appendix 8A. Listing follows a reported wildlife strike, and Tarlton Springs has never been listed. The investigation is examining that criterion and why no inspection was requested before SB512 departed.

4. Approval of the evening service. CMR-2025-118 introduced the evening rotation as a schedule change only. It did not reopen the daylight-only restriction in route risk assessment RRA-041. SB512 began its roll after the end of civil twilight, and the investigation is examining how the change was assessed.

5. The rejected take-off and the stop. The decision to reject 6 kt above V1 is being examined against Operations Manual Part B 4.6 and recurrent training syllabus TRG-SYL-07. Neither contains a runway obstruction scenario. The stopping performance is also being examined, as the strike left reverse thrust on No. 1 only.

6. Cabin floor escape path lighting. A functional check found two floor proximity segments on VH-SBQ dark because of a failed ballast. The pre-flight test does not cover them. Engineering is examining whether the part is fitted across the fleet.

Two matters remain open. One is whether the flight crew were fatigued at take-off. The other is whether four northern edge lights, measured above nominal intensity seven days later, affected when the animals were seen.

# Containment and next steps

Three interim actions have been taken since the occurrence. They are reported as actions taken in the interim, not as evidence of what caused it.

- The Head of Flying Operations suspended the evening Tarlton Springs service on 19/06/2026. [NOT ESTABLISHED: whether the suspension remains in place] - On 26/06/2026 the Head of Flying Operations amended Appendix 8A by temporary revision TR-2026-04 to list Tarlton Springs. The Part A 8.14.2 runway inspection now applies to every departure there after last light, pending a permanent change. This action is in place. - The aerodrome operator has funded works register item WR-221 from its 2026 contingency. A temporary electric wire was installed on 25/06/2026. Replacement of the 140 m northern boundary fence section is programmed for 09/2026, and this action is still being implemented.

The investigation has also recommended that the operator change the Appendix 8A listing criterion and add a route risk assessment trigger for any aerodrome first operated into after last light. A response was due by 30/09/2026, and the recommendation remains open. No safety action has been recorded on training and guidance for a runway obstruction, the approval of the evening service, the evening rotation roster, or floor escape path lighting.

The investigation's next steps are:

- Engineering is establishing whether the failed ballast part is fitted across the fleet, and how many aircraft are inside the 24-month test interval now. - The investigation is still examining whether the four northern edge lights affected when the animals were seen. - Someone outside the investigation will review the draft report. The people and organisations it describes will then be invited to correct matters of fact.

Review notes

Observations the model made about the record itself while drafting, kept apart from the prose for the investigator to resolve.

- [What we know] - Disembarkation timeline entry (07:34:40 to 07:38:48, "4 m 08 s"): the notification says passengers were disembarked 4 minutes 8 seconds after the aircraft stopped at 07:30:57, which would be about 07:35:05. The timeline instead runs 4 minutes 8 seconds from the airstair operating at 07:34:40, ending at 07:38:48, which is about 7 minutes 51 seconds after the stop. The two readings are incompatible. The draft states what each source records. - Scaffold line for 07:30:33 says the No. 2 propeller was "destroyed". The inspection report records three blades damaged beyond repairable limits, which is not the same thing. The draft follows the inspection report. - Scaffold line for 07:30:02 says the roll began "five minutes after the end of civil twilight". The source material refers only to "last light" (a causal-chain factor title) and to darkness. Civil twilight is not mentioned anywhere, so the draft says darkness only. - Scaffold line for 07:29:10 expands AWIS as "automatic weather broadcast". The source never gives that expansion, so the draft keeps AWIS as written. - Runway departure speed: the notification gives approximately 21 kt, while the FDR row at 07:30:51 gives 21 kt IAS and 20 kt groundspeed. The draft uses the notification's approximate figure. - Number of animals struck: the notification and inspection (two carcasses, tissue in two blade roots) support both being struck. The pilot in command's CVR remark and safety report refer to striking "at least one". The draft follows the physical evidence. - [What we are looking at] - Four northern edge lights above nominal intensity on the night: the factor carries "Led to: Take-off rejected 6 kt above V1", and the reject lists it under "Led from", yet its Influence test is Inconclusive (about as likely as not). An inconclusive factor carrying a causal link to the reject looks inconsistent with how the fatigue factor, also inconclusive, was deliberately left with no link. - Flight crew fatigued at the time of the take-off: the category is given as "Hypothesis (untested)", but the Existence test was argued and recorded as Inconclusive, and the Influence reasoning was set out in the test note. "Untested" does not match what was done. - No training or guidance for a runway obstruction during the take-off roll: Existence is recorded as "extremely likely". Under the reporting scale this narrows to "very likely" wherever it is graded in the final report. - [Containment and next steps] - Recommendation on the Appendix 8A listing criterion: the response was due 30/09/2026 and the status is still "Open". The current date is past that due date, so the record may not reflect whether a response has been received. - Fence safety action (WR-221): the replacement was programmed for 09/2026, but the status is still "In progress" as at 25/06/2026. The record does not show whether the replacement has been completed. - Investigation log, 19/06/2026: the evening Tarlton Springs service was suspended, but the record holds no later entry on whether it has resumed. TR-2026-04 is framed around departures there after last light, which suggests resumption may have been contemplated. The current status needs confirming. - Floor escape path lighting: this object is still in draft pending the fleet ballast answer from engineering, and no target date for that answer is recorded. - [Summary] - Summary outline "Date" line: it gives 17/06/2026, but the critical event, the notification and every factor place the occurrence on 18/06/2026 (07:30 UTC, 17:30 local). The draft uses 18/06/2026. The 17/06/2026 date appears to come from the morning inspection (21:35 UTC on 17/06/2026, 07:35 local on 18/06/2026) and should be corrected. - Summary outline "Safety issues" list: it carries the roster factor and the floor escape path lighting factor with the term "unlikely". That is their failed Influence grade. Their Importance grades are "likely" and "very likely". Taken out of context, the term misstates them as unlikely safety issues. - "Contributing factors" list: it tags the fence factor and the wildlife-plan threshold factor as safety issues, as well as the factors listed under safety issues. It also gives "extremely likely" for two factors without narrowing them to "very likely".

  • Summary length: the specification allows three or four sentences, and the draft runs to five. The investigator may wish to merge sentences or trim the open-questions sentence, which repeats what the earlier section already covers.
