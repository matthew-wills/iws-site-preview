What happened

On 07/07/2026, MV Tarrakin Star was operating service TF18. She is a 24.4 m aluminium catamaran passenger ferry certificated for 96 passengers. TF18 was the last service of the day, an evening return from Muntari Island. The ferry departed at 17:35 with 38 passengers, a master, a deckhand and a kiosk attendant on board. Sunset had been at 17:02 and civil twilight had ended at 17:29, so the voyage was made in darkness. This had been true on every day the service had run since the winter timetable began on 01/06/2026, and this was the thirty-first such voyage.

Earlier that afternoon, the master had changed at the Kanooka Heads town wharf. The outgoing master told the relieving master that the chart plotter (the electronic chart display) had frozen twice that morning. Both times it had recovered by itself.

The ferry entered the Merrandi Channel from the north at 18:03:58. She held the charted centreline to within 3 m at 8.4 kt. Shortly afterwards, the deckhand was sent from the bridge to collect fares. At 18:07:06 the chart plotter froze again, and this time it did not recover. The vessel symbol stayed stationary on screen, and the display would not accept touch input. In the same second, the vessel's heading began to ease to starboard. At 18:08:31 the master began looking for the No. 4 beacon (a channel marker), and he sighted it 91 seconds later. Later checks found that mangroves screen the beacon from the channel between 0.84 nm and 0.34 nm north of it. By about 18:09 the ferry had crossed the western edge of the dredged channel on a held heading of 199 degrees. She was still making 8.0 kt, and no throttle movement was recorded.

At 18:11:48 the echo sounder shallow alarm sounded. The alarm had been set to warn at 0.9 m under the keel since the vessel was commissioned in 2019, and it gave 16 seconds' warning. Both throttles were put astern 4 seconds later. On trials, a crash stop from 8 kt takes 92 m, but only 58 m remained. At 18:12:04 the ferry struck Boolarong Bank at 6.8 kt. She was 74 m west of the centreline and 54 m outside the dredged channel. She stopped within 11 m and came to rest heeled 4 degrees to starboard on a falling tide.

The engines were stopped at 18:12:31, and the decision was made not to try to refloat. An urgency broadcast went to Port Control at 18:13:04, and Port Control called out the port workboat. All passengers were in lifejackets by about 18:22, although a locker behind the kiosk counter delayed the forward saloon. The workboat came alongside at 18:41, and the last passenger was ashore at 19:26.

There were no serious injuries. One passenger reported a grazed shin, and two were treated at the wharf for distress. The starboard hull was split, and a void flooded to approximately 260 mm. The starboard propeller, shaft and rudder were damaged, and the skeg was torn away. The ferry refloated at 00:20 and was withdrawn from service. The channel was closed to commercial traffic for 19 hours.

Why it happened

The ferry grounded because it drifted out of the dredged channel and was still travelling at 8 kt when the warning came, leaving too little room to stop. Its heading eased five degrees to starboard without any decision to turn, and it was virtually certain that this took the vessel out of the channel on the bank side.

The sequence began with the chart plotter freeze. It was extremely likely that the freeze contributed to the heading easing to starboard. It was also extremely likely that the freeze contributed to the transit continuing at 8 kt from that moment until the shallow alarm sounded. The plotter, moreover, had a history. The operator's defect procedure never required a fault that cleared itself to be recorded (Safety issue), and this likely contributed to the plotter freezing during the transit.

With the plotter lost, the No. 4 beacon was the obvious reference ahead. Mangroves, however, screened it from the channel between 0.84 nm and 0.34 nm north of it, and this likely contributed to the heading easing to starboard. Behind the screening lay an organisational cause. Clearing of the beacon's sightline had been deferred, unfunded, for three works rounds (Safety issue). It was very likely that this deferral contributed to the beacon being screened.

The speed was the second part of the problem. There was no night passage plan, and no hold point told the master what to do if the primary position fix was lost. It was very likely that this absence contributed to the ferry continuing at 8 kt. This gap, too, traced back to a decision made earlier in the year. The last service had been moved into darkness without the daylight-only risk assessment being reopened (Safety issue), and this likely contributed to there being no night passage plan or hold point.

Once the ferry was outside the channel, little margin remained. It was virtually certain that leaving the channel contributed to the shallow alarm giving only 16 seconds' warning, and extremely likely that the continued 8 kt did so as well. The alarm had been set at 0.9 m since 2019. It was extremely likely that this setting, and the short warning it produced, contributed to the grounding.

Two further matters were examined but could not be settled. The first concerns the unshielded boat ramp floodlights, which were aligned behind the No. 4 beacon when seen from the north. The alignment was real, and the masters' accounts of it were consistent and independent. Even so, the investigation could not establish either way whether the floodlights delayed the sighting in the seconds after the light cleared the islet, and so delayed the moment the held heading was noticed. It assessed this as about as likely as not. Whatever the answer, a lighting assessment of the channel is warranted.

The second concerns fatigue. Whether the master was fatigued was also about as likely as not. His duty and sleep made fatigue plausible, and he acknowledged being tired. The recorded performance, however, showed no sign of it. No detailed fatigue assessment was made. Every decision that mattered on the night was either explained by another factor or contradicted by the recorded data.

The investigation also identified other matters that increased risk but did not contribute to this grounding. These are reported with the findings.

What we found

A finding stated without a likelihood term was established as fact.

Contributing safety factors

  • It was virtually certain that the last service was moved into darkness without the daylight-only risk assessment being reopened, and this likely contributed to the grounding. (Safety issue)
  • It was virtually certain that there was no night passage plan and no hold point for loss of the primary fix (the main means of fixing the vessel's position), and it was very likely that this absence contributed to the grounding. (Safety issue)
  • The operator's defect procedure never required a fault that cleared itself to be recorded, which was virtually certain, and this likely contributed to the grounding. (Safety issue)
  • The chart plotter display froze during the channel transit and did not recover, which was virtually certain, and this very likely contributed to the grounding.
  • With virtual certainty, the transit continued at eight knots from the plotter freeze until the shallow alarm, and this very likely contributed to the grounding.
  • Clearing of the beacon's sightline was deferred, unfunded, for three works rounds, which was virtually certain, and this very likely contributed to the grounding. (Safety issue)
  • The No. 4 beacon was screened by mangroves from the channel between 0.84 and 0.34 nm north of it, which was virtually certain, and this likely contributed to the grounding.
  • The heading eased five degrees to starboard without a decision to turn, and this contributed to the grounding. Both were virtually certain.
  • That the vessel left the dredged channel on the bank side was virtually certain, as was its contribution to the grounding.
  • The shallow alarm at its 2019 setting of 0.9 m gave sixteen seconds' warning, which was virtually certain, and this very likely contributed to the grounding.

Other factors that increased risk

  • The aids code set no notice or availability duty for beacons off declared channels, which was virtually certain. This did not contribute to the grounding, but it likely increased risk. (Safety issue)
  • The beacon's range was halved by a solar conversion with no notice to channel users, which was virtually certain. Although this did not contribute to the grounding, it likely increased risk. (Safety issue)
  • The darkness transit was rostered to close a split duty within 20 minutes of the spread limit, which was virtually certain. This did not contribute to the grounding, but it likely increased risk. (Safety issue)
  • The forward saloon lifejacket locker was blocked by a counter fitted in 2019 without re-survey, which was virtually certain. While this did not contribute to the grounding, it very likely increased risk. (Safety issue)
  • At the change of master, the plotter fault was passed on verbally and not entered in the defect book, which was virtually certain. This did not contribute to the grounding, but it likely increased risk.

Other findings

  • The investigation examined whether the tide set down below its predicted height over Boolarong Bank and ruled it out, finding it very unlikely that any such set-down existed.
  • A steering or propulsion failure before the vessel left the centreline was examined and ruled out, and it was found very unlikely that any such failure occurred.
  • After the contact the engines were stopped and no attempt was made to refloat on a falling tide, which was virtually certain, and this very likely reduced risk.

Review notes

Observations the model made about the record itself while drafting, kept apart from the prose for the investigator to resolve.

  • [What happened] - Contact timeline entry (18:12:04) cites "the survey of 08/07/2026", but the survey and slipping report is dated 09/07/2026 to 14/07/2026.
  • Factor "Unshielded boat ramp floodlights aligned behind the No. 4 beacon from the north" appears as the head of a causal chain to the heading change, yet it is listed as having failed the Influence test.
  • Factor "Heading eased five degrees to starboard" against the recorded course of 195 degrees at the No. 2 beacon and a held heading of 199 degrees at the channel edge, which is a four-degree difference. This may be a course versus heading distinction, but it should be confirmed.
  • Critical event evidence describes the furrows as running 11 m "on a heading of 197 degrees", while the timeline records a held heading of 199 degrees. This may also be a track versus heading distinction and is worth confirming.
  • [Why it happened] - "Unshielded boat ramp floodlights aligned behind the No. 4 beacon from the north": the drafting instruction lists this factor among those that "did not pass the Influence test". The record shows its Influence result as Inconclusive (about as likely as not), not Fail. The text treats it as unresolved and does not place it upstream of the grounding.
  • "Unshielded boat ramp floodlights aligned behind the No. 4 beacon from the north" is marked Finalised: no, yet it also carries a "Led to: Heading eased five degrees to starboard" link. That link is inconsistent with an Inconclusive Influence result.
  • "Forward saloon lifejacket locker blocked by a 2019 counter fitted without re-survey" is marked Finalised: no. Its categorisation may still change.
  • "Master fatigued during the channel transit": the note says whether he "was rostered into that position is tested on the roster object". The roster safety issue failed Influence (unlikely), so that question appears to be closed on the roster object rather than left open.
  • [What we found] - "Darkness transit rostered to close a split duty within 20 minutes of the spread limit", "Master fatigued during the channel transit" and "Heading eased five degrees to starboard without a decision to turn": the investigator notes date the split or addition to 09/10/2026, but the material was generated on 2026-10-08. The date runs ahead of the generation date.
  • "Chart plotter display froze during the channel transit and did not recover", "Transit continued at eight knots" and "Shallow alarm at its 2019 setting": the analysis graded the influence of all three as extremely likely. Under the findings-scale rule this has been narrowed to very likely here. The approved "Why it happened" section, however, uses "extremely likely" for these factors, so the two sections now differ in wording.
  • "No night passage plan or hold point for loss of the primary fix": this factor sits under "Safety issues (organisational level)" and is tagged as a safety issue in this section. The approved "Why it happened" section did not tag it, while it did tag the other three organisational safety issues.
  • Checklist, Interviews: the item asking whether each interview has been written up, checked against the record and finalised is marked Yes. The deckhand's interview record is marked as not finalised.
  • [What is being done] - Approved "What we found": the chart plotter freeze and the continued transit at eight knots are each stated as having "very likely" contributed. In the source material both carry Influence "Pass - extremely likely".
  • Approved "What happened": the held heading is given as 199 degrees. The source material for the heading change and the channel departure gives 200 degrees, and the course over ground came back from 200 to 197 degrees.
  • Investigator notes on "Darkness transit rostered..." and "Master fatigued..." are dated 09/10/2026 ("Split out of the old roster object"). The note on "Heading eased five degrees..." is dated "Added 09/10/2026". All three dates are later than the material's generation date of 2026-10-08.
