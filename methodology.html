Methodology

Findings should be derived, not declared.

An investigation in IWS starts from the critical event and works backwards. Every event or condition that might explain it goes on the AcciMap as a hypothesis, each is tested against the evidence, and the results classify it. The findings follow from those classifications, and the record keeps the evidence and reasoning behind each step, so anyone reviewing the work can see how a finding was reached.

The work is not one pass from left to right. Evidence that settles one test can also point to a hypothesis nobody had considered, or show that one on the map is worded wrongly. The investigator adds or revises it and tests again, and the classifications follow the latest results.

Not every classified object becomes a finding. An event or condition with no bearing on risk, and an object whose tests are not yet settled, stay out of the report's Findings section. What each classification becomes

2The AcciMap

Start at the critical event and work backwards

The critical event goes on the AcciMap first, in Occurrence Events, conventionally at the bottom right. The investigation then works backwards from it: what happened just before, what conditions were present, which risk controls should have caught it, and what in the organisation and beyond shaped those controls. Objects run left to right in time.

Every hypothesis goes on at this stage, including the ones expected to fail. An excluded hypothesis stays on the map, struck through, as part of the record.

An arrow is a claim that one thing led to another. It stays grey and dashed until the object it comes from has passed both Existence and Influence, then turns solid.

The six lanes, top to bottom

  • External Influences regulators, sector standards, oversight and guidance
  • Organisational Influences the organisation's own systems, such as hazard recognition and management of change
  • Risk Controls the defences that should prevent or catch the occurrence
  • Local Conditions the conditions on the day: weather, workload, fatigue, knowledge and experience
  • Individual Actions what people did
  • Occurrence Events the event sequence itself, converging on the critical event
3The three tests

Each object is asked up to three questions, in order

Existence

“Did this event or condition actually exist or occur?”

Asked first, of every object, the critical event included. A fail excludes a hypothesis.

Influence

“Did it influence this occurrence?”

Asked only once Existence passes. Had it not existed, would the occurrence probably not have happened, or been less severe?

Importance

“Is it important enough to address anyway?”

Asked only where Influence fails. Would addressing it reduce the likelihood or severity of a future occurrence?

The critical event’s Existence is established through evidence, like any other object’s: the investigation has to show that it happened. In place of Influence it records the proof of its significance, unless it leads on to a later occurrence, when the form asks “Did it lead to the subsequent occurrence?” It has no Importance test.

  • Conclusive or argued, on any test. Where the evidence is not in dispute, one summary is enough. Where it cuts both ways, the test is argued: the evidence for and against are recorded side by side, and the probability reflects the weighing.
  • The probability sets the result. The investigator records how probable the proposition is, and IWS reads it against the standard of proof: Pass, Fail or Inconclusive. How the boundaries work
  • Not tested is not the same as not settled. A test can be left Untested, or marked Insufficient evidence where it was examined and the evidence supports no probability, which reads as Inconclusive.
  • A note on each result says why it went the way it did, and the investigator's notepad holds the wider reasoning.
4Classification

The results and the risk direction decide what an object is

When an object is placed, the investigator says which way the hypothesis bears on risk: increased risk, decreased risk or neutral. Only an increased-risk object can become a contributing safety factor or an other risk factor; a pass on its own does not make one. The classification is what the object is on the AcciMap; whether the report lists it as a finding is a separate step that follows from it.

5Probability and the standard of proof

How probability shapes the analysis

The investigator assigns a probability to each test's proposition. IWS interprets that probability against the selected standard of proof to give the test's result, and the test results contribute to how each object is classified.

How IWS interprets the selected probability

The standard of proof is 70 per cent unless it is changed in Settings, from 50 to 95 per cent in steps of 5. It is set per computer and applies to every test.

  • Pass at or above the standard.
  • Fail at or below 100 minus the standard: 30 per cent or less at the default.
  • Inconclusive in between. At a standard of 50 the two meet, and 50 itself passes.

The standard of proof determines how an assessment is classified; it does not determine whether the investigator's assessment is sound.

Expressing probability consistently

IWS provides ten defined probability terms, so the same word means the same thing in every investigation. The boundaries, and other approved wording for a term, such as “probably” for likely, can be set in Settings.

A report's Findings section uses seven of the ten terms, to reduce ambiguity. The three marked * are stated as their neighbour there: extremely likely as very likely, more likely than not as likely, and exceptionally unlikely as extremely unlikely.

6Reporting

The working behind each finding, written out

The Evidence and Argument Tables are generated directly from the assessments recorded against AcciMap objects. After the AcciMap itself, each object has its own table, grouped under its lane in the order the map puts them: each test's result and probability, the argument for and against, and the reasoning that says which side carried.

Nothing is typed twice, and the tables come with every licence level, Free included. The reports draw on the same record, with their Findings sections built from the classifications above.
